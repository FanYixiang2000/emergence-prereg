# Cover letter (draft for NMI submission)

Dear Editors,

We submit our manuscript "Emergence as collapse of the effective joint
possibility space" for consideration as an Article in Nature Machine
Intelligence.

**The problem is real and unresolved.** "Emergence" is currently
certified with a different instrument in every field -- order
parameters in physics, success-rate jumps in language models, visual
inspection of behaviour in multi-agent learning -- and recent work has
shown that some celebrated emergent abilities are artefacts of the
metric rather than properties of the system. The field lacks a
definition that is measurable, falsifiable, and shared across systems.

**What we do.** We define emergence as a spontaneous, persistent,
regime-level collapse of a system's effective joint
state-action-trajectory possibility space -- what the system's members
can still jointly do, rather than what they are doing -- and we build
a preregistered, frozen instrument that measures when that space
closes, how abruptly, and through which channel (environmental,
individual, pairwise, or higher-order). Every threshold was fixed
before the experiments it judges; every prediction carries a
falsification clause; every miss is reported in the paper.

**The central finding** is a timing law that current metrics cannot
see: in learning populations with several equally good joint
conventions, the possibility space collapses before task competence
matures. Systems first commit to who they will be, then learn to do
it well. The commitment moment carries practical value: remaining
openness predicts, prospectively and on unseen runs, whether an
intervention can still change the outcome.

**The evidence spans** analytic ground truth (72-cell factorial
calibration), classic physics (Kuramoto, Potts, Vicsek, ant
collectives with a finite-size law), and learned systems: convention
formation and role lock-in with tabular and neural policies (25 of 27
learned seeds carry a certified onset, and in every certified onset
the collapse precedes the capability crossing), a mechanism-recovery
experiment inside the standard Overcooked benchmark, and negative
results wherever the theory predicts none -- ordinary smooth
optimization collapses gradually, and our instrument says so.

**Why NMI.** The paper speaks directly to the emergent-abilities
debate, to multi-agent RL where emergent coordination is claimed by
inspection, and to the control question -- when is a learning system
still steerable -- that we believe will matter increasingly for AI
oversight. The tooling (a standardized emergence certificate with an
explicit "unresolvable" verdict) is released for direct community use.

All data are generated by the released code; the manuscript's numbers
are reproduced by a single extraction script from the raw outputs.
The work is not under consideration elsewhere.

Sincerely,
The Authors
